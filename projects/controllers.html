02 / The controller I built

A wooden shell.
A lot of solder joints.

The original uses mechanical switches, mostly clear keycaps, a Raspberry Pi Pico and hand-soldered wiring. A transparent plexiglass bottom panel lets you see the electronics through the base. The reconstruction keeps the 22-key layout and four mismatched black caps visible in my photographs.

Some switches have started coming loose. The next enclosure should hold them securely and make the electronics easier to access. My parts include Kailh BOX Silent Pink switches and a Treedix USB-C breakout.

03 / A possible next enclosure

BubbleBox.
A printed alternative.

BubbleBox is an open controller design by UMS-Ultra. Its printed shell incorporates the switch seats; a separate bottom panel closes the enclosure. The view separates those parts so the construction is easier to understand.

This is a design exploration, not a second completed build. The display uses the standard V4 mono case. Its USB arrangement is shown schematically; adapting it for my Treedix board remains future work.

Sources & model notes

A view inside, with the assumptions visible.

The wooden enclosure uses 360.68 × 165.10 × 21 mm, matching f1copy's documented dimensions, as confirmed for this build. Key positions were traced from photographs. The transparent plexiglass bottom panel is confirmed from the actual build. Panel thicknesses, interior mounting and wire routes are reconstructed; this is a display model, not a fabrication drawing.

The BubbleBox shell and bottom panel are simplified from UMS-Ultra / BubbleBox, specifically the V4 RH Standard mono case and mono bottom panel. The derived model is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. Keycaps, switches, sockets, electronics and screws are illustrative. The teal finish is a visualization choice.
